Only a handful of studies demonstrate the existence of hysteresis in bistable systems. In a follow-up from our earlier work, we study the recovery trajectory of a light-stressed plankton population in a chemostat experiment ([early view in Oikos](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/oik.02006/abstract)). We find that reverse trajectories can be explained by hysteresis, time-delays and adaptive process, all of which pose interesting questions for the behavior of bistable systems under changing conditions.

Recently, we summarized a set of measures that can be used as [spatial indicators for detecting loss of resilience](/news/2014-04-17_spatial-methods-of-early-warnings-for-tipping-points.md "Spatial methods of early warnings for tipping points").  We  now add another measure that can be used as early warning of critical transitions in spatially explicit systems: spatial heteroskedasticity. In short, this indicator is the analog of [conditional heteroskedasticity](/news/2012-09-19_plos-one-paper-out.md "PLoS One paper out!") in timeseries (the non constant variance along a timeseries). We now expand its use from indicator of critical transitions in timeseries to spatial data ([early view in Ecology & Evolution](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ece3.1510/abstract)).
